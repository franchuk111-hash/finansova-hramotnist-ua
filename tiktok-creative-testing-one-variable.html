Creative testing on TikTok: three variants, one variable, seven days

Оновлено 27 October 2026 · Business Ads Guide

The fastest way to waste a month on TikTok is to test five creatives that differ in hook, format, offer and CTA at the same time. Whichever wins, you cannot say why — and you cannot repeat it deliberately. One variable per round is slower on paper and dramatically faster in practice.

The structure

One ad group, three creatives, the same landing page, the same audience. Run for seven days or until the ad group reaches about 50 conversions, whichever comes first. Choose the variable before you brief the creatives: hook (first two seconds), first frame, CTA wording, or offer. Everything else stays identical, including the ad format.

Signals worth reading

Hook rate — three-second views divided by impressions — tells you whether the opening works. Hold rate shows whether the middle survives. CTR shows intent, and conversion rate shows whether the promise matches the landing page. Compare every metric to your own account baseline, never to a benchmark post from a different vertical. If conversion tracking is not firing correctly, all four numbers are fiction.

Kill rules

After 1,000 impressions, a creative below half of baseline CTR is dead — stop spending on it. A creative at 1.5× baseline CTR with CPA under target is the new control. Anything between the two stays until the end of the test window; killing it early is how accounts end up with no data and a strong opinion.

One more guardrail: with three creatives and roughly 50 conversions a week, each variant gets about 17 — enough to rank them loosely, not enough to declare a 10% difference significant. Treat the round as a shortlist and confirm the winner in the next test rather than rebuilding the account around a coin flip.

Then iterate on the winner's hook, not on the whole ad. Three hooks against one winning body beats three entirely new ads, because you keep the part that already converts. The written breakdowns in the blog follow the same rule — one variable, a stated baseline, a verdict.
