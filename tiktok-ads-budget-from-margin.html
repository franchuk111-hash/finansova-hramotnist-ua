How to build a TikTok ads budget from your own margin

7 November 2026 · Business Ads Guide

Start with the platform floor, then ignore it

TikTok's documentation currently states that campaign daily and lifetime budgets must exceed US$50, and ad-group daily budgets must exceed US$20. The ad-group lifetime threshold is the minimum daily amount multiplied by scheduled days. These are floors, not recommendations: a campaign that clears them can still be unaffordable for your unit economics.

Contribution margin decides the ceiling

Take revenue after discounts and refunds, then subtract the variable costs that grow with each sale: delivery, payment fees, fulfilment. What remains is the contribution available to pay for acquisition. For a hypothetical US$60 order at a 65% contribution margin, contribution is US$39. Spend more than US$39 to acquire one order and that order does not recover its advertising cost. The 65% figure is an example input, not a TikTok benchmark.

Size the test, not the month

Choose one business outcome, an affordable maximum loss, and a written review date. Estimate CPA from your own past data, or record a clearly labelled assumption if none exists. Multiply expected CPA by the number of conversions you need to observe. Wanting 30 orders at an assumed US$30 CPA implies US$900 in media spend; adding US$300 in creative and setup costs brings the campaign to US$1,200.

Why daily and lifetime are not interchangeable

Daily budgets are described as an average daily amount; lifetime budgets allocate a total across the run. Neither is a promise of even delivery or a fixed number of sales, and TikTok does not allow switching between the two after publication. Decide which model fits your review cadence before you launch, not after.

The full worked example, with the threshold sources and the contribution-margin table, is on TikTok Ads budget planning. It is written as a planning tool: the outputs are hypothetical, not campaign results.

Set the review cadence before launch

Pick a date and a rule. For example: review after 40 conversions or 14 days, whichever comes first, and stop if CPA exceeds the affordable maximum. A written rule prevents the two most expensive behaviours in small accounts — extending a losing test because spend already happened, and scaling a winner before there is enough data to trust it.

Keep creative production and measurement setup in a separate line of the budget. When those costs hide inside media spend, every CPA comparison silently overstates performance.
