First-loan promotions: how to test whether the offer is really cheaper

12 November 2026 · Loans247 ZA

A promotion is a condition, not a discount

First-loan offers in South Africa usually take one of two forms: a reduced interest rate for the first agreement, or a fee waiver. Both are real, and both come with conditions that determine whether you actually receive the benefit. The most common condition is repayment on or before the due date. Miss it by a day and the concession can fall away retroactively, which means the interest you thought you avoided is charged for the entire period.

That single clause is why two borrowers with the same loan can pay very different amounts. It is not a trick in the illegal sense — it is disclosed in the quotation — but it is easy to miss when the marketing says "0%" in large type.

Worked comparison

Take R4,000 over 30 days. Offer A advertises no interest for first-time borrowers but applies the standard rate for the whole term if payment is late. Offer B charges a normal rate from day one with no conditions. If you pay on time, A costs less. If you pay three days late, A can cost more than B — sometimes 25–40% more, because the penalty is calculated on the full term rather than on the days overdue. If your income date is not fixed, B is the safer choice even though it looks worse in the advert.

So the test is not "which headline is lower". It is "which offer survives my worst realistic month". Write down the date your money actually lands, not the date it is supposed to land, and compare against that.

What must appear in the quotation

Principal, term, interest rate and interest type, initiation fee, service fee, credit life insurance if required, the total amount repayable, and the consequences of late payment. If any of these is missing from the pre-agreement quotation, the promotion cannot be evaluated — and an offer you cannot evaluate is not an offer you should accept.

The affordability question comes first

None of this matters if the repayment does not fit your budget. Lenders in South Africa are required to assess affordability, and you should run the same check yourself: after rent, transport, food and existing debt, what is left? If the answer requires you to skip a payment somewhere else, the promotion is irrelevant — the loan is too large.

Borrow the smallest amount that solves the problem, for the shortest term you can service. Promotions reward discipline, and discipline starts with the amount you choose, not with the rate you are offered.
