How to read a Sri Lankan loan quotation: five lines that change the cost

12 November 2026 · Loans247 LK

The figure that matters is the last one

Loan quotations in Sri Lanka are usually presented with a monthly figure, because a monthly figure is what a borrower can compare to income. The number that determines whether the loan is affordable is the total amount repayable, and it is frequently printed in smaller type. Read the document from the bottom up: total repayment first, then term, then the rate, and only then the instalment.

Two quotations with identical monthly instalments can differ by a third in total cost if the terms differ. Once you know the total, the instalment becomes an output rather than a decision.

Flat versus reducing balance

A flat rate is calculated on the original principal for the whole term. A reducing balance is calculated on the outstanding amount, so it falls as you repay. A flat rate of 12% is not comparable to a reducing rate of 12% — the flat version can cost close to twice as much over the same term. If the quotation does not say which method is used, ask. The answer changes the comparison entirely, and any lender that will not answer in writing is telling you something useful.

Fees outside the rate

Processing or documentation fees, credit evaluation charges, insurance bundled as a condition, and the penalty for late payment. Each has to be in the quotation. A fee deducted upfront means you receive less than you borrow while repaying the full amount, which raises the effective cost above the stated rate. For a small short-term loan this can be the single largest cost component.

Term: the quiet variable

Extending the term lowers the instalment and raises the total. That is arithmetic, not salesmanship, and it is where most borrowers accidentally overspend: the instalment fits, so the loan feels affordable, and the extra months are invisible. If you need a lower instalment, ask whether a smaller principal achieves the same thing. It usually does, and it costs less.

A five-minute routine

Write down: amount received, total repayable, term, interest method, and the late-payment penalty. Compare those five across two or three licensed providers. Keep the written quotation. If a provider will not give you one before you sign, stop — under any regulated framework the quotation is a pre-contract document, not a courtesy.

Borrowing decisions are usually made under time pressure, which is exactly why the checklist should be mechanical. The five lines take five minutes and routinely save more than the difference between the best and worst advertised rate on the market.
