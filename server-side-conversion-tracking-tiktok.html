Conversion tracking without the pixel: server-side events for TikTok

Оновлено 4 November 2026 · Business Ads Guide

What the pixel misses

A browser pixel fires from the user's device. If an ad blocker, Safari's tracking prevention or a consent banner blocks it, the conversion still happens — it just never reaches TikTok. Reported losses range from 15% to 30% depending on audience and geography, and they are not evenly distributed: iOS users are underrepresented in pixel data, which skews optimisation toward Android.

How server-side events work

Instead of the browser calling TikTok, your server sends the event directly through the Events API. The browser may still send a pixel event; TikTok deduplicates the pair using a shared event_id. The server copy survives ad blockers because it never touches the client.

The part people get wrong: matching

Server-side events only count if TikTok can match them to a user. That requires hashed identifiers — email, phone, external_id — sent alongside the event. Skip the hashing step and your match rate collapses to 20–30%, which looks like a tracking outage. Hash with SHA-256, normalise emails to lowercase and strip phone formatting first.

What to expect after switching

Reported conversions usually rise 10–25% in the first two weeks. That is not new revenue — it is recovered attribution. CPA will look worse and ROAS better, because the denominator and numerator both move. Judge the change against a control period of at least 14 days, not against the day before launch.

Setup steps and required fields are documented in TikTok conversion tracking. If you are still running pixel-only, start with the TikTok pixel guide — the event naming conventions carry over directly.
