Broad targeting vs interest targeting on TikTok: when each one wins

Оновлено 5 листопада 2026 · BusinessAdsGuide

Interest targeting feels safer: you pick the categories that match your product and assume the algorithm finds the right people. In practice, on TikTok, broad targeting beats interest targeting in most accounts above a modest budget — but not in all of them.

Why broad usually wins

TikTok's auction is creative-led. The system learns who converts from the creative and the first few hundred conversions, not from the interest tags. Interest stacks also shrink the pool fast: three overlapping interests can cut reach by 70% while adding no precision.

Accounts that switch from a 4-interest stack to broad typically see CPM fall 15-25% and CPA fall 10-20% within two weeks — provided the creative is specific enough to self-select the audience.

When interest targeting still makes sense

  • Very small budgets where the algorithm has no room to explore.
  • Regulated categories with hard audience restrictions.
  • Products with a narrow, identifiable buyer where creative cannot signal the audience (B2B software, industrial equipment).
  • Retargeting and lookalike layers, which are not "interest" in the same sense.

The creative-specificity rule

Broad works when the first two seconds of the video make the audience obvious. A video that opens with "if you run a salon in a city of 200k, this is your ad" self-selects better than any interest stack. A generic brand video on broad targeting burns budget on everyone.

A test that answers the question in 10 days

  1. Duplicate the winning ad group twice.
  2. Variant A: broad, no interests, age 18-65.
  3. Variant B: your current interest stack.
  4. Same creative, same budget, 10 days, no edits.
  5. Compare CPA at equal spend, not at equal impressions.

Most accounts find broad wins on cost and interest wins on early conversion rate — and cost matters more once volume scales.
