TikTok Ads: What the First 30 Days Should Actually Look Like

Оновлено 6 November 2026 · Business Ads Guide

Week one is for signal, not scale

The first seven days exist to answer one question: can this account buy a conversion at a cost that leaves room for profit? That means one campaign, one objective, three to five ad groups, and a daily budget high enough to exit the learning phase — typically 20 to 50 conversions per ad group per week. If your budget cannot reach that, you are not testing, you are sampling noise.

Week two: kill the losers early

By day ten you will have enough data to cut. Turn off ad groups with a cost per acquisition more than 1.5x your target and no improving trend. Do not pause a group because one day looked bad; look at the seven-day rolling average. The most common first-month mistake is keeping twelve ad groups alive on a budget that supports three.

Week three: isolate the variable

Now you change one thing at a time — creative, audience, or landing page. Changing two at once tells you that something worked, never what. If a new creative doubles CTR but CPA stays flat, the problem is downstream: the landing page or the offer, not the ad.

Week four: decide, don't drift

At day thirty you should be able to state a number: this account acquires a customer for X, and the margin supports Y. If X is above Y after four weeks of honest testing, the answer is not a bigger budget. The full sequencing logic is laid out in our TikTok ads strategy guide.

Before you commit the second month's budget, run your own numbers through the ROI calculator — it forces the margin assumption into the open, which is where most first-month plans quietly fall apart.
